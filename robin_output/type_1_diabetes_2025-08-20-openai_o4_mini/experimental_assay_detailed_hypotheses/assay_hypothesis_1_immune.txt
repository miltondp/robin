Assay Overview:  
This assay co‐cultures human iPSC‐derived or primary β cells with autologous CD8+ T cells under defined antigen presentation conditions to recreate the HLA class I–restricted killing observed in type 1 diabetes (T1D). The β cells, which can be derived from patient-specific iPSCs or primary tissue, are pre‐treated with inflammatory cytokines (e.g., IFN-γ, TNF-α) to induce the upregulation of HLA class I and other immune checkpoint molecules, thereby mimicking in vivo inflammatory conditions (Clinical Trial Search: be12e83dc7d7, castrogutierrez2021protectingstemcell pages 1-2). In parallel, autologous CD8+ T cells are introduced, and their cytotoxic response is measured by rapid readouts such as lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) release or caspase-3/7 activity assays, providing quantitative measures of β‐cell death. Complementary to these endpoints, multiplex cytokine profiling (e.g., for IFN-γ and TNF-α) is performed to assess T cell activation and Th1 inflammatory signaling, thus offering a comprehensive evaluation of the T cell–β cell interaction (Clinical Trial Search: be12e83dc7d7, castrogutierrez2021protectingstemcell pages 6-8).

Biomedical Evidence:  
Autoimmune destruction of pancreatic β cells by autoreactive CD8+ T cells is a central mechanism in T1D pathogenesis, with HLA class I–restricted antigen recognition playing a pivotal role in directing immune-mediated β cell cytotoxicity (Clinical Trial Search: be12e83dc7d7). The assay models this aspect by recapitulating not only the cell-to-cell contact necessary for antigen-specific T cell activation but also the subsequent release of proinflammatory cytokines that drive disease progression (castrogutierrez2021protectingstemcell pages 1-2, leite2020modelingtype1 pages 4-5). Clinical studies and immunotherapy trials in T1D have underlined the importance of modulating such immune responses; thus, a platform that can accurately measure both direct β cell death and cytokine secretion profiles is highly relevant for early-stage drug discovery (NCT03895996, leite2020modelingtype1 pages 4-5).

Previous Use:  
Several studies have utilized immune co-culture cytotoxicity assays to elucidate the mechanisms of diabetogenic T cell recognition and β cell destruction. For instance, experiments using sBC or iPSC-derived β cells with diabetogenic CD8+ T cells have demonstrated antigen-specific T cell activation, as reflected by increased IL-2 and other cytokine secretion, and validated strategies for immune evasion such as HLA class I knockout or PD-L1 overexpression (castrogutierrez2021protectingstemcell pages 6-8, castrogutierrez2021protectingstemcell pages 9-10, castrogutierrez2021protectingstemcell pages 1-2). These approaches have provided valuable insights into how modulating antigen presentation and T cell degranulation can protect β cells, thereby informing the development of immunomodulatory compounds (leite2020modelingtype1 pages 1-4, castrogutierrez2021protectingstemcell pages 8-9).

Overall Evaluation:  
Strengths of this assay lie in its physiological relevance and translational potential. By employing human iPSC-derived or primary β cells, the system better represents the cellular context of T1D compared to animal models (Clinical Trial Search: be12e83dc7d7). Additionally, the dual readout strategy—combining direct cytotoxicity measurements (via LDH or caspase activity assays) with multiplex cytokine profiling—offers a robust means to monitor both cell death and the inflammatory milieu, which are crucial endpoints for screening immunomodulatory drugs (Clinical Trial Search: be12e83dc7d7, pqac-30d5e8c8).  
However, challenges include potential variability in both iPSC differentiation protocols and the quality of primary β cells, which may affect assay reproducibility. Moreover, establishing optimal antigen presentation conditions and ensuring appropriate HLA matching in autologous co-cultures are critical for mimicking the in vivo scenario accurately (leite2020modelingtype1 pages 16-17). It may be necessary to complement these assays with additional methods, such as flow cytometry or high-content imaging, to confirm the mode of cell death and to dissect the specific contributions of different cytokine signals (NCT03895996, leite2020modelingtype1 pages 4-5).  

In conclusion, immune co-culture cytotoxicity assays offer a highly promising platform for evaluating candidate therapeutics aimed at mitigating β cell destruction in T1D. Their ability to model the key autoimmune interactions, quantify rapid cytotoxicity events, and profile cytokine responses provides a comprehensive tool for early-stage drug discovery and validation in the context of T1D (Clinical Trial Search: be12e83dc7d7, castrogutierrez2021protectingstemcell pages 1-2).
