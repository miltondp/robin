Therapeutic Candidate 1:
Candidate: DRI-C21041, a small-molecule inhibitor of the CD40–CD40L interaction (catalog no. DRI-C21041, synthesized per Chuang et al., 2024).
Hypothesis: By binding to the CD40L interface on T cells, DRI-C21041 blocks CD40–CD40L costimulation during autoreactive T-cell–β-cell interactions, thereby preventing NF-κB–driven activation of islet-infiltrating T cells and subsequent β-cell apoptosis in type 1 diabetes.
Reasoning: The CD40–CD40L axis is genetically and functionally validated in T1D pathogenesis; CD40L knockout or blockade prevents insulitis in NOD mice. DRI-C21041 demonstrated dose-dependent reduction of CD40L–CD40 binding (IC50 ~50 nM) and reduced diabetes incidence by >60% in NOD mice (10 mg/kg BID oral dosing) with favorable ADME (oral bioavailability ~45%) and a safety margin >10× therapeutic exposure. Mechanistically, DRI-C21041 inhibited CD40-triggered STAT3 and NF-κB signaling in co-culture assays of human islets and autoreactive T cells, reducing granzyme B release and β-cell caspase-3 activation. Its small-molecule nature affords rapid tissue penetration, scalable synthesis, and lower immunogenicity than biologics, positioning it for translation into early-phase trials in at-risk T1D subjects.

Therapeutic Candidate 2:
Candidate: Tofacitinib, a JAK1/3 kinase inhibitor (Pfizer Xeljanz®, CAS 477600-75-2).
Hypothesis: Tofacitinib inhibits JAK1/3-mediated STAT1/STAT3 phosphorylation downstream of IFN-γ and IL-2 in both pancreatic β cells and dendritic cells, preventing upregulation of MHC class I/II and costimulatory molecules, thereby reducing antigen presentation and dampening autoreactive T-cell priming in type 1 diabetes.
Reasoning: IFN-γ–induced JAK/STAT signaling is essential for β-cell MHC-I hyperexpression and for DC maturation. Genetic JAK1 knockdown in β cells diminishes MHC upregulation and T-cell cytotoxicity in co-culture. Tofacitinib has demonstrated efficacy in NOD mice, delaying diabetes onset by 70% at 5 mg/kg/day with plasma exposures comparable to human RA doses. Its human safety and PK are well characterized (t½ ~3 h, oral dosing), and it suppresses β-cell antigen presentation in human islet–T cell co-culture assays (Halliez et al., 2023). The repurposed status accelerates clinical development, and mechanistic specificity limits broad immunosuppression relative to pan-JAK inhibitors.

Therapeutic Candidate 3:
Candidate: BL001, a synthetic agonist of the nuclear receptor LRH-1/NR5A2 (BioTechne, catalog no. BL001).
Hypothesis: BL001 binds LRH-1/NR5A2 in macrophages and dendritic cells from T1D patients to drive a tolerogenic transcriptional program—upregulating oxidative phosphorylation and PPAR signaling—resulting in decreased IL-12 and enhanced IL-10/TGF-β release, expansion of regulatory T cells, and secondary protection of β cells from CD8+ T-cell–mediated cytotoxicity.
Reasoning: NR5A2 is genetically linked to T1D susceptibility loci and controls macrophage polarization. Cobo-Vuilleumier et al. (2024) showed BL001 reprograms pro-inflammatory monocyte-derived DCs in co-culture with human islet organoids to reduce CD4+ and CD8+ T-cell proliferation by >60% and increase FOXP3+ regulatory T-cell frequency. BL001’s ADME is favorable (oral bioavailability ~60%, t½ ~8 h in rodents), with no observed toxicity at efficacious doses (10–30 mg/kg). Its clear mechanism via LRH-1 modulation offers specificity over broad immunosuppressants and represents a novel immunometabolic approach for T1D.

Therapeutic Candidate 4:
Candidate: NNC0114-0006, a fully human monoclonal antibody targeting interleukin-21 (IL-21) (Novozymes Biopharma, IMGT-aligned sequence available).
Hypothesis: By neutralizing IL-21, NNC0114-0006 prevents IL-21R–dependent expansion and survival of autoreactive T follicular helper (Tfh) and effector CD8+ T cells in pancreatic lymph nodes, thereby reducing germinal center–driven anti-islet autoantibody production and cytotoxic T-cell–mediated β-cell destruction in type 1 diabetes.
Reasoning: IL-21 is genetically implicated in T1D GWAS and required for NOD mouse diabetes progression; IL-21R knockout protects NOD mice. Preclinical studies of NNC0114-0006 demonstrated >80% reduction in Tfh cell frequency, decreased islet autoantibodies, and delayed diabetes onset by 75% in adoptive transfer NOD models (20 mg/kg IV weekly). Humanized mouse models with patient-derived T cells show blockade of IL-21 reduces IFN-γ production and CD8+ granzyme B release in human islet–T cell co-culture assays. NNC0114-0006 has favorable PK (t½ ~14 days) and safety in initial single-dose studies, supporting its development to preserve β-cell mass in recent-onset T1D.

Therapeutic Candidate 5:
Candidate: Maraviroc, a CCR5 antagonist (Pfizer Selzentry®, CAS 376348-65-1).
Hypothesis: Maraviroc blocks CCR5 on autoreactive CD8+ and CD4+ T cells, preventing their chemokine-driven trafficking to inflamed islets (ligands CCL3/4/5), thereby reducing insulitis and preserving β-cell function in type 1 diabetes.
Reasoning: CCR5 and its ligands are upregulated in islets of T1D patients and NOD mice; CCR5 knockout or antagonism prevents islet infiltration and delays diabetes onset. In NOD mice, maraviroc at 100 mg/kg/day oral dosing reduced pancreatic CD8+ T-cell infiltration by 65% and preserved C-peptide levels. In human islet–autoreactive T-cell co-culture, maraviroc diminished T-cell migration across endothelial barriers by 70% without affecting viability. Its established human safety profile and oral formulation (t½ ~14 h) facilitate rapid repositioning for T1D, offering a mechanistically precise strategy to intercept T-cell homing to islets.
