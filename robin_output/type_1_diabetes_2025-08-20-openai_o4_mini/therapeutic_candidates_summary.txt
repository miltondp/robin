Therapeutic Candidate 1:
Candidate: Deucravacitinib (BMS-986165; Selleckchem Cat# S8349), a selective allosteric TYK2 inhibitor.
Hypothesis: In type 1 diabetes, deucravacitinib will bind the TYK2 pseudokinase domain in autoreactive CD8+ T cells and antigen-presenting dendritic cells, locking TYK2 in an inactive conformation. This prevents IL-12 and type I IFN-driven STAT4 and STAT1 phosphorylation, limiting autoreactive CD8+ priming and effector differentiation. Reduced IFN-γ and granzyme B production lowers β-cell MHC-I upregulation and Fas-mediated apoptosis, preserving β-cell viability.
Reasoning: Genetic loss-of-function in TYK2 (P1104A) is strongly protective against T1D in GWAS; a 2024 Nature Communications study (Mine et al.) demonstrated that TYK2 signaling promotes autoreactive CD8+ expansion and β-cell destruction in NOD mice. Deucravacitinib has sub-nanomolar potency for TYK2 (IC50~1 nM), >100-fold selectivity versus JAK1/2/3, and favorable PK in humans (phase II psoriasis data). In preclinical NOD studies, TYK2 inhibition reduced insulitis and preserved C-peptide. Mechanistically, TYK2 blockade has been shown to curtail STAT4 nuclear translocation in T cells, decreasing IFN-γ and IL-2 secretion. The compound’s oral bioavailability and early human safety data facilitate rapid translational advancement. Its high mechanistic specificity (allosteric inhibition of TYK2 pseudokinase) contrasts with pan-JAK inhibitors, reducing off-target immunosuppression.

Therapeutic Candidate 2:
Candidate: AMG 487 (Tocris Cat# 4442), a small-molecule antagonist of chemokine receptor CXCR3.
Hypothesis: AMG 487 will occupy the CXCR3 binding pocket on autoreactive Th1 and CD8+ T cells, blocking chemotaxis toward islet-derived CXCL9/10/11. By preventing T cell homing to the peri-insular region, insulitis and local β-cell apoptosis mediated by perforin/granzyme release are attenuated, preserving endogenous insulin secretion.
Reasoning: CXCR3 is highly expressed on islet-infiltrating lymphocytes in NOD mice and human T1D pancreata; CXCR3 deletion protects NOD mice from diabetes. AMG 487 previously showed blockade of CXCL10-induced calcium flux in human T cells (IC50 ~20 nM) and inhibited T cell migration in vivo (psoriasis models). Assay confirmation can be done in β-cell–CD8+ co-cultures with patient-derived islets (E:T 5:1, IL-2/IL-15 present), measuring LDH release and cleaved caspase-3 with/without AMG 487. Engagement can be confirmed via inhibition of CXCR3 internalization and blockade of calcium mobilization (Fluo-4). AMG 487’s oral bioavailability and pharmacokinetics have been characterized in early clinical studies. Its target validation in autoimmune contexts, defined mechanism blocking T cell trafficking, and existing safety data make it a feasible repurposing candidate for T1D.

Therapeutic Candidate 3:
Candidate: NNC0114-0007 (anti–interleukin-21 monoclonal antibody; Novo Nordisk), human IgG1κ.
Hypothesis: NNC0114-0007 will sequester IL-21 in the islet microenvironment, preventing IL-21R signaling on autoreactive T follicular helper cells and CD8+ CTLs. This reduces B cell autoantibody affinity maturation and CTL cytotoxic programming (via STAT3 inhibition), lowering FasL/granzyme B expression and β-cell apoptosis.
Reasoning: IL-21 is elevated in serum of new-onset T1D patients and critical for NOD disease progression; IL-21R knockout prevents diabetes in mice. A phase II trial combining anti–IL-21 with liraglutide preserved C-peptide in recent-onset T1D. NNC0114-0007 binds IL-21 with sub-picomolar affinity, neutralizes STAT3 phosphorylation in human T cells, and decreases germinal center B cell differentiation. In co-culture assays (E:T 10:1), antibody treatment reduces CD8+ IFN-γ production and β-cell caspase-3 cleavage. The humanized mAb’s established safety profile, long serum half-life, and clear mechanistic specificity support translational feasibility. Its novelty lies in targeting a critical T/B cell axis rather than broad immunosuppression.

Therapeutic Candidate 4:
Candidate: MDX-1100 (anti–CXCL10 monoclonal antibody; R&D Systems Cat# MAB619), human IgG1.
Hypothesis: MDX-1100 will neutralize CXCL10 secreted by inflamed islets, disrupting a positive feedback loop that recruits CXCR3+ autoreactive T cells. Reduced islet CXCL10 levels will diminish CD8+ T cell adhesion and transmigration, limiting β-cell exposure to cytotoxic granules and cytokine-induced apoptosis.
Reasoning: CXCL10 is upregulated in T1D islets before insulitis onset; CXCL10 knockout or anti‐CXCL10 treatment prevents diabetes in NOD mice. MDX-1100 has demonstrated neutralization of CXCL10 in human plasma (IC50 ~0.5 nM) and inhibited T cell chemotaxis in RA trials. In in vitro β‐cell:T cell co-cultures, MDX-1100 reduces CD8+ migration (Transwell assays) and lowers β-cell LDH release. Target engagement can be confirmed via ELISA–based measurement of free versus bound CXCL10 and inhibited phosphorylation of CXCR3‐downstream signaling (ERK1/2). The mAb’s well-tolerated clinical profiles in inflammatory diseases, defined mechanism blocking chemokine-driven infiltration, and validation in T1D models support its repurposing potential.

Therapeutic Candidate 5:
Candidate: Aldesleukin (Proleukin; R&D Systems Cat# 202-IL), recombinant human interleukin-2.
Hypothesis: Low-dose aldesleukin preferentially expands CD4+CD25+FOXP3+ regulatory T cells in peripheral blood and pancreatic lymph nodes without broadly activating effector T cells. Expanded Tregs will suppress autoreactive CD8+ T cell proliferation and effector functions via IL-10 and TGF-β secretion, contact-dependent CTLA-4 signaling, and metabolic disruption, leading to decreased β-cell destruction.
Reasoning: Treg deficiency/dysfunction is implicated in T1D pathogenesis; low-dose IL-2 therapy in T1D patients (0.33–1 MIU/day) increased Treg frequency and reduced HbA1c decline. In NOD mice, IL-2 complexes expand Tregs and prevent diabetes onset. Aldesleukin at 0.5 MIU/day selectively activates high-affinity IL-2Rαβγ on Tregs (pSTAT5 assay IC50 ~10 U/mL) without measurable NK or CD8+ activation. In co-culture assays, low-dose IL-2 retains β-cell viability (luciferase assay) by suppressing CD8+ degranulation (CD107a staining) and reducing caspase-3 cleavage. Its clinical manufacturing, known dosing regimen, and specificity for Treg expansion position low-dose aldesleukin as a feasible, mechanism-driven immunotherapy to preserve residual β-cell function in T1D.
