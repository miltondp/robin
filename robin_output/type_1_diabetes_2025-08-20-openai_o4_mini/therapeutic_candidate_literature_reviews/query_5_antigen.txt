Pancreatic β cells play an active role in type 1 diabetes (T1D) pathogenesis by engaging in antigen presentation, a process traditionally associated with professional antigen‐presenting cells. Under inflammatory conditions, β cells upregulate both MHC class I and – in certain contexts – MHC class II molecules, thereby increasing their capacity to present endogenous peptides, including neoantigens generated from defective ribosomal products and post‐translational modifications (kracht2016neoantigensandmicroenvironment pages 97-99, li2021revisitingtheantigenpresenting pages 1-2). In vitro human β‐cell assays have demonstrated that exposure to proinflammatory cytokines such as IFN-γ and IL-1β leads to hyperexpression of HLA class I, which not only enhances direct presentation of native and spliced autoantigens to CD8+ T cells but also facilitates the formation of an immune synapse (kracht2016neoantigensandmicroenvironment pages 97-99, ren2024researchprogressof pages 7-9).

Mechanistic dissection using iPSC-derived β cells and primary human islet cells has revealed that inflammatory stress and ER stress substantially alter the antigen processing machinery. Such stress triggers an upregulation of MHC molecules alongside modifications in peptide repertoire, including neoepitope generation via alternative splicing and post-translational modifications (e.g., deamidation and citrullination) (kracht2016neoantigensandmicroenvironment pages 47-54, kracht2016neoantigensandmicroenvironment pages 63-67). These neoantigens are critical in breaking self-tolerance, thereby priming autoreactive CD8+ and CD4+ T cells to attack β cells. In vitro assays substantiate that stressed β cells exhibit a robust capacity to process and present these peptides, directly linking intracellular stress responses to autoimmunity in T1D (kracht2016neoantigensandmicroenvironment pages 176-178, li2021revisitingtheantigenpresenting pages 8-8).

Although β cells are not professional antigen-presenting cells, under inflammatory conditions they display an APC-like phenotype that includes the expression of accessory molecules. For instance, cytokine stimulation can induce the surface expression of adhesion molecules such as ICAM-1, which facilitates effective immune synapse formation with T cells by stabilizing cell–cell interactions (li2021revisitingtheantigenpresenting pages 6-7, li2021revisitingtheantigenpresenting pages 2-3). While classical co-stimulatory molecules (e.g., CD80/CD86) are either minimally expressed or absent on normal β cells, their inducible expression under stress—and interactions with professional APCs—ensures delivery of co-stimulatory signals required for full T cell activation (kracht2016neoantigensandmicroenvironment pages 29-30, li2021revisitingtheantigenpresenting pages 1-2). Thus, the convergence of antigen processing, MHC regulation, and co-stimulatory signaling at the β cell surface constructs an immune synapse that is integral to the activation of autoreactive T cells.

Detailed in vitro mechanistic studies also illustrate that the inflammatory milieu typical of T1D further escalates this process. Cytokines such as IFN-γ and TNF-α not only enhance MHC expression but also modulate antigen processing pathways within β cells. This intrinsic response reinforces a local environment that favors immune recognition and cytotoxic T cell-mediated β cell destruction (pugliese2017autoreactivetcells pages 2-3, kracht2016neoantigensandmicroenvironment pages 77-80). Moreover, these studies suggest that β cell-derived neoantigens, when presented in the context of an inflammatory microenvironment, contribute to a diversification of the autoreactive T cell repertoire and a breakdown of peripheral tolerance (kracht2016neoantigensandmicroenvironment pages 176-178, pugliese2017autoreactivetcells pages 3-5).

In summary, mechanistic dissection through in vitro human β-cell assays confirms that pancreatic β cells actively participate in antigen presentation during T1D. Inflammatory and stress signals drive upregulation of MHC class I (and in some cases class II) molecules, enhance neoepitope generation, and promote the expression of co-stimulatory and adhesion molecules necessary for immune synapse formation. These coordinated events enable a direct interaction between β cells and autoreactive T cells, ultimately fostering the autoimmune cascade that underlies β cell destruction in T1D (kracht2016neoantigensandmicroenvironment pages 97-99, li2021revisitingtheantigenpresenting pages 1-2, pqac-8e484bf0, pqac-f62b91b6, pqac-04dac83d).
