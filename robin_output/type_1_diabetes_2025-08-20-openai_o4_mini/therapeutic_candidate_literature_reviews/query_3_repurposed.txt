A comprehensive search using the defined query on ClinicalTrials.gov revealed no registered clinical studies that combine repurposed drugs, novel small molecules, or biologics for type 1 diabetes therapy with immune co‐culture cytotoxicity or islet–T cell co‐culture assay data alongside pharmacokinetic/ADME or human safety information and concurrent mechanism‐of‐action or pathway modulation analyses (Clinical Trial Search: 2a7205c76376).

Despite the absence of such integrated clinical trial records, robust preclinical evidence exists. Detailed mechanistic studies using the synthetic LRH-1/NR5A2 agonist BL001 have addressed several facets of the query. In these investigations, BL001 was shown to reprogram fully activated pro‐inflammatory macrophages and myeloid dendritic cells from T1D patients into an anti‐inflammatory, tolerogenic state. These effects were evaluated in immune co‐culture systems—specifically, assays where islet‐like organoids derived from human iPSCs were co‐cultured with peripheral blood mononuclear cells. The data indicated that BL001 significantly reduced CD4+ and CD8+ T‐cell proliferation while expanding regulatory T‐cell populations. Mechanistically, the compound induced mitochondrial hormesis, increased mitochondrial turnover, and modulated key immune pathways including PPAR signaling and oxidative phosphorylation (OXPHOS), thereby establishing a clear pathway modulation in its mode of action (cobo‐vuilleumier2024lrh‐1nr5a2targetsmitochondrial pages 1-4, cobo‐vuilleumier2024lrh‐1nr5a2targetsmitochondrial pages 25-27).

Additional preclinical evidence comes from studies on biologic approaches such as alpha-1 antitrypsin overexpressing mesenchymal stem/stromal cells (AAT-MSCs). These studies employed islet–T cell co‐culture assays to assess immunomodulatory effects. The results demonstrated that AAT-MSCs promoted regulatory T-cell function and induced CD8+ T-cell exhaustion, effectively mitigating islet cell cytotoxicity. This work, although preclinical and lacking direct human safety data from clinical trials, reinforces the potential of cell-based biologics to exert immune tolerance mechanisms in T1D (wei2025alpha1antitrypsinoverexpressing pages 25-29).

In contrast, several reviews and clinical trial reports on immunotherapies in T1D—such as those involving anti-CD3 antibodies (teplizumab), abatacept, and B-cell depletion strategies—have provided substantial human safety data and insights into pharmacokinetic profiles. However, these studies generally do not incorporate specific immune co‐culture cytotoxicity assays or detailed mechanistic evaluations that combine these endpoints with pharmacokinetic assessments (creusot2016concisereviewcellbased pages 4-5, liu2021therapeuticadvancesin pages 51-53).

In summary, while current registered trials do not integrate the full spectrum of immune co‐culture cytotoxicity or islet–T cell co‐culture assays with pharmacokinetics/human safety and mechanism‐of‐action analyses for repurposed drugs, novel small molecules, or biologics in T1D therapy, preclinical studies demonstrate that this multifaceted approach is feasible. The evidence from mechanistic studies using BL001 and AAT-MSCs highlights the potential for such integrated methodologies to translate into clinically meaningful strategies. These promising results advocate for future clinical trial designs that will incorporate comprehensive immune co‐culture assays alongside detailed pharmacokinetic and mechanistic evaluations, ultimately bridging the gap between preclinical insights and therapeutic validation in type 1 diabetes (pqac-1cd27a66, cobo‐vuilleumier2024lrh‐1nr5a2targetsmitochondrial pages 1-4, cobo‐vuilleumier2024lrh‐1nr5a2targetsmitochondrial pages 25-27, wei2025alpha1antitrypsinoverexpressing pages 25-29).
