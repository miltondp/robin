Recent preclinical studies have integrated high‐throughput screening assays with immune co‐culture cytotoxicity systems to elucidate the mechanisms underlying beta‐cell destruction and to evaluate therapeutic strategies in type 1 diabetes. These platforms typically combine autologous or autoreactive T cells with pancreatic β cells derived from either primary sources or human pluripotent stem cells. Using such models, researchers quantitatively assess T cell‐mediated cytotoxicity through a variety of readouts, including CD107a mobilization, granzyme and perforin secretion, and caspase‐3 activation, providing sensitive and early indications of β‐cell apoptosis (halliez2023invitrobetacell pages 9-11).

Some studies have optimized these assays to not only monitor cytotoxicity but also to establish mechanistic links between immune recognition and β‐cell survival. For example, modified β cells—such as those deficient in renalase—demonstrate an attenuated activation profile in co‐cultures with HLA‐mismatched peripheral blood mononuclear cells and exhibit upregulation of immunomodulatory checkpoint molecules like TGFβ1, CD47, and CEACAM1. This modification leads to decreased NK and T cell cytotoxicity and improved graft survival in diabetic rodent models, thereby illustrating target engagement and mechanistic confirmation in an in vivo context (bode2024betacellsdeficient pages 8-10).

Additional approaches leverage stem cell–derived islets that are genetically manipulated to model human autoimmune responses. These studies employ CRISPR genome editing to disrupt T1D risk genes and to modify HLA class I expression or enhance PD-L1 expression on β cells. Such genetic modifications effectively decrease the recognition and subsequent cytotoxicity mediated by autoreactive CD8+ T cells. By using T cell lines, clones, or TCR‐transduced primary T cells in a co‐culture format, these platforms reproduce key elements of autoimmune β‐cell killing, and mechanistic confirmation is achieved by demonstrating specific T cell activation cascades, including IL‐2 secretion and upregulation of activation markers (halliez2023invitrobetacell pages 4-7, castrogutierrez2021protectingstemcell pages 1-2).

In parallel, models utilizing primary human CD8+ T cells or TCR‐transduced cells have provided additional mechanistic insights. For instance, immortalized T‐cell lines, despite some limitations, have been instrumental in delineating early T cell receptor signaling events upon contact with β cells. More physiologically relevant approaches, wherein primary T cells are expanded from peripheral blood or engineered to express islet‐reactive TCRs, show robust effector functions and better recapitulate the in vivo immune milieu. The co‐culture platforms thus serve as a robust system to confirm target engagement—whether by monitoring changes in cytokine profiles or by establishing the involvement of specific antigen‐presentation pathways—and pave the way for subsequent preclinical animal model validations (pqac-03e632f1, halliez2023invitrobetacell pages 9-11).

Preclinical efficacy has been further validated in animal models. For example, adoptive transfer experiments in nonobese diabetic mouse models have demonstrated that β cell preparations rendered less immunogenic (via genetic modulation of HLA molecules or immune checkpoint proteins) prolong graft survival and improve diabetic phenotypes. These studies attest to the translational potential of in vitro screening assays by linking mechanistic target engagement to beneficial outcomes in vivo (lai2024traj23genetargeting pages 4-6).

Furthermore, review articles and high-throughput studies provide a broader perspective by integrating these immune co‐culture methods with advanced imaging platforms and multiparametric analyses to monitor β‐cell function and immune interactions. The cumulative evidence across these diverse studies supports that high‐throughput screening assays incorporating immune co‐culture cytotoxicity with autologous or autoreactive T cells offer a highly specific and clinically relevant framework for identifying and validating novel therapeutic targets, thereby informing strategies to preserve pancreatic β cell integrity in type 1 diabetes (halliez2023invitrobetacell pages 16-16, halliez2023invitrobetacell pages 11-12, halliez2023invitrobetacell pages 1-3).
