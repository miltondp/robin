Assay Candidate 1:
Strategy: MHC‑I Modulation Assay
Reasoning: This assay tests the hypothesis that reducing interferon‑γ‑driven MHC‑I up‑regulation on β‑cells will lower their visibility to autoreactive CD8⁺ T cells. iPSC‑derived β cells are pre‑treated with a small‑molecule STAT1 inhibitor, then exposed to a diabetogenic cytokine cocktail (IL‑1β, TNF‑α, IFN‑γ). Surface MHC‑I is quantified by flow cytometry, while concurrent Annexin V/TUNEL staining assesses apoptosis. A significant drop in MHC‑I without loss of insulin secretion (measured by GSIS or Gaussia luciferase) indicates that the drug can dampen antigen presentation while preserving β‑cell function. This approach directly links a mechanistic target—STAT1‑mediated MHC‑I induction—to a functional readout in a human‑derived system, building on evidence that IFN‑γ enhances MHC‑I (Query 1, Ref 1) and that flow‑based MHC‑I assays are established for β‑cells (Query 3, Ref 5).

Assay Candidate 2:
Strategy: Cytokine‑Shielding Small‑Molecule Screen
Reasoning: This strategy evaluates compounds that can block the pro‑inflammatory cascade initiated by β‑cell‑derived cytokines while preserving insulin secretion. β‑cells (EndoC‑βH1 or iPSC‑derived) are exposed to a 24‑hour cocktail of IL‑1β, TNF‑α, and IFN‑γ, followed by treatment with a library of antioxidant or immunomodulatory molecules. Multiplex ELISA (AlphaLISA) measures IL‑10, IL‑1β, TNF‑α, IFN‑γ in the supernatant, and simultaneous high‑content imaging tracks intracellular Ca²⁺ flux and insulin granule exocytosis. A therapeutic that lowers pro‑cytokines, boosts IL‑10, and maintains Ca²⁺‑mediated insulin release demonstrates dual anti‑inflammatory and pro‑survival activity. The design mirrors the high‑throughput platform described by McCarty et al. (2024) and addresses the key readouts identified in comprehensive T1D assays (Query 3).

Assay Candidate 3:
Strategy: CRISPRi Loss‑of‑Function Screen for Immune Resistance
Reasoning: This proposal uses a genome‑wide CRISPR interference library delivered to iPSC‑derived β cells, followed by co‑culture with activated CD8⁺ T cells. Cells are sorted by live‑cell imaging for survival (non‑apoptotic Annexin V⁻, caspase‑3⁻) and then sequenced to identify guides enriched in the resistant population. Coupling the screen with RNA‑seq of surviving β cells provides transcriptional signatures of immune evasion (e.g., down‑regulated MHC‑I, up‑regulated immune checkpoint ligands). This loss‑of‑function approach directly tests the hypothesis that specific β‑cell genes (e.g., those highlighted by Grotz et al. 2021) confer resilience to cytotoxic attack. The strategy leverages established CRISPR screens (Ref 7) and integrates key readouts—apoptosis, MHC‑I, and transcriptomics—recommended for T1D therapeutic evaluation (Query 3).
