Assay Overview:  
This proposal employs a genome‑wide CRISPR interference (CRISPRi) loss‑of‑function screen in induced pluripotent stem cell‑derived β cells that are subsequently co‑cultured with activated CD8⁺ T cells. The assay is designed to model the autoimmune destruction of β cells in type 1 diabetes (T1D) by directly assessing the cells’ intrinsic capacity to resist cytotoxic T‑cell attack. The experimental setup uses a pooled CRISPRi library to partially suppress gene expression across the genome in β cells, thereby allowing identification of genes whose down‑regulation confers resistance to immune-mediated apoptosis. The endpoints are measured via live‑cell imaging incorporating viability markers (Annexin V⁻ and caspase‑3⁻ staining) to sort surviving cells, followed by next‑generation sequencing of enriched guide RNAs. Concomitant RNA‑sequencing of surviving β cells provides a transcriptional signature that highlights alterations in immune evasion phenotypes, such as reduced MHC‑I expression or increased expression of immune checkpoint ligands (karpov2023challengesofcrisprcasbased pages 19-20, Clinical Trial Search: 045e862b8797).

Biomedical Evidence:  
T1D pathogenesis is driven by autoimmune destruction of insulin‑producing β cells largely mediated by autoreactive CD8⁺ T cells. In the clinical and biomedical literature, dysregulation of β‑cell-intrinsic pathways that govern antigen presentation and apoptotic sensitivity has been implicated in disease progression (OpenTargets Search: Type 1 diabetes). The assay focuses on these critical immune interaction pathways by measuring markers such as MHC‑I expression and immune checkpoint ligand levels, along with assessing cell survival in the face of cytotoxic challenge. This approach is highly relevant because prior studies have demonstrated that β‑cell modifications—whether by genetic engineering or epigenetic regulation—can modulate sensitivity to immune attack (karpov2023challengesofcrisprcasbased pages 1-2). Furthermore, transcriptomic profiling of surviving cells can reveal adaptive changes in gene expression that potentially mirror the immune evasion strategies observed in vivo, lending further clinical relevance to the findings (memon2022towardprecisionmedicine pages 7-8).

Previous Use:  
Genome‑wide CRISPR screens, including those using CRISPRi, have been successfully applied in various disease models to identify genes linked to immune resistance. Although direct reports of CRISPRi loss‑of‑function screens using iPSC‑derived β cells co‑cultured with CD8⁺ T cells are not yet documented, related methodologies in cancer and autoimmunity have identified key regulators of immune evasion through similar co‑culture assays (yi2021applyingcrisprscreen pages 8-8, castrogutierrez2021protectingstemcell pages 9-10). Additionally, other studies have used genome‑wide CRISPR knockout screens and genetic engineering approaches in β cells to uncover protective targets that favor graft survival in autoimmune contexts (jang2023genomeeditingfor pages 5-7, cai2020genomescaleinvivo pages 1-2). These prior discoveries underscore the potential of this approach not only for target identification in basic research but also as a platform for early‑stage drug discovery in T1D.

Overall Evaluation:  
Strengths of this assay include its human cell relevance through the use of iPSC‑derived β cells and its ability to capture multiple facets of T1D pathogenesis—ranging from apoptotic responses to antigen presentation dynamics—via multiplexed readouts. The CRISPRi approach permits tunable gene suppression, thereby avoiding complete loss‑of‑function that could be detrimental to cell viability and mirroring more closely the pharmacologic inhibition seen in therapeutic contexts (karpov2023challengesofcrisprcasbased pages 19-20). Furthermore, coupling phenotypic screening with transcriptomic analysis enriches the dataset and may reveal novel pathways for therapeutic intervention (colli2020molecularfootprintsof pages 8-9).  
Weaknesses include the inherent limitations of in vitro co‑culture models in recapitulating the full complexity of the in vivo autoimmune milieu. The assay may not fully account for additional immune cell subsets or systemic factors that influence β‑cell destruction in T1D (memon2022towardprecisionmedicine pages 7-8). Moreover, variability in the maturity and heterogeneity of iPSC‑derived β cells could complicate data interpretation, and CRISPRi’s ability to achieve complete gene suppression can be variable among targets (yi2021applyingcrisprscreen pages 4-6, Clinical Trial Search: 045e862b8797). Despite these challenges, the assay represents a compelling and innovative strategy for early‑stage therapeutic evaluation and mechanistic dissection of immune resistance in T1D.
