Query: Describe state‑of‑the‑art in vitro cell culture systems for type 1 diabetes drug testing, including human iPSC‑derived beta‑cell lines, primary human and mouse islet cultures, 3D organoid and islet‑on‑chip platforms, co‑culture models with autoreactive T cells or dendritic cells, CRISPR‑edited islet cells, and high‑throughput screening‑compatible microplate assays for insulin secretion and immune activation

State‑of‑the‑art in vitro cell culture systems for Type 1 diabetes drug testing integrate multiple advanced platforms that recapitulate the complexity of islet biology, autoimmunity, and beta‐cell function. Recent developments combine human induced pluripotent stem cell (iPSC)‑derived beta‑cell lines, primary islet cultures, three‐dimensional (3D) organoid and islet‑on‑chip platforms, immune cell co‑cultures, CRISPR‑edited islet cells, and high‑throughput microplate assays into a multifaceted toolkit for mechanistic studies and therapeutic screening (maxwell2021applicationsofipscderived pages 4-5, OpenTargets Search: Type 1 diabetes).

Human iPSC‑derived beta‑cell lines now represent a robust and scalable platform for modeling T1D. These cells are differentiated via multi‐step protocols that mimic pancreatic development, yielding stem cell beta‑like cells that gradually acquire a mature gene expression profile—including upregulation of markers such as PAXBP‑1, GLS2, and FXYD2, along with appropriate functional glucose‐stimulated insulin secretion (GSIS) (maxwell2021applicationsofipscderived pages 4-5, OpenTargets Search: Type 1 diabetes). Advances in re‑aggregation methods and optimized media have enhanced mitochondrial and secretory functions, producing cells with dynamic insulin release properties resembling adult beta‑cells (beydagtasoz2023towardsabetter pages 13-14, beydagtasoz2023towardsabetter pages 6-7). Moreover, these platforms are amenable to genetic manipulation via CRISPR/Cas9, allowing correction or introduction of disease‑associated mutations in genes such as WFS1, HNF1A, PDX1, ZnT8, GLIS3, and INS, thereby enabling precise disease modeling and drug target validation (beydagtasoz2023towardsabetter pages 8-10, maxwell2021applicationsofipscderived pages 4-5).

Primary human and mouse islet cultures remain essential as benchmark models given their preservation of native cellular heterogeneity and architecture. These primary islets offer a reference for assessing beta‑cell maturity, phenotypic markers, and insulin secretory responses, and are critical for validating the physiological relevance of iPSC‑derived counterparts (thiab2025humaninducedpluripotentstem pages 26-27, OpenTargets Search: Type 1 diabetes). Detailed cellular and molecular characterizations in these cultures have underscored beta‑cell dysfunction and the specific responses observed in diabetic conditions, providing a basis for comparative analyses with engineered systems (OpenTargets Search: Type 1 diabetes).

Three‑dimensional organoid systems and islet‑on‑chip platforms represent a transformative advance in modeling the in vivo pancreatic microenvironment. 3D organoid models, generated from either iPSC‑derived cells or primary islets, form pseudoislets that better replicate the native cell–cell and cell–extracellular matrix interactions compared to conventional two‑dimensional cultures (beydagtasoz2023towardsabetter pages 13-14, beydagtasoz2023towardsabetter pages 6-7). Furthermore, islet‑on‑chip platforms incorporate microfluidics and dynamic perfusion systems to simulate interstitial flow, precise glucose challenges, and real‑time insulin monitoring via sensitive detection methods. These microfluidic devices not only improve measurements of insulin secretion dynamics but also support multi‑parameter analyses such as calcium signaling and oxygen consumption, enhancing the physiological relevance and throughput in drug screening (mohandas2023newfrontiersin pages 16-18, oliveira2023type1diabetes pages 10-12, beydagtasoz2023towardsabetter pages 3-5).

Co‑culture models integrating immune cells, including autoreactive T cells or dendritic cells, further elevate the physiological fidelity of these in vitro systems by replicating the autoimmune interactions central to T1D pathogenesis. By assembling iPSC‑derived beta cells or primary islets with immune effectors, researchers can study antigen presentation, cytokine signaling, and immune‑mediated beta‑cell destruction, offering insight into disease mechanisms and immunomodulatory drug responses (pqac-ecaaf828, OpenTargets Search: Type 1 diabetes).

CRISPR‑edited islet cells provide an additional layer of precision, permitting targeted manipulation of key genetic determinants implicated in monogenic or autoimmune diabetes. Through CRISPR/Cas9‐mediated editing, researchers can introduce mutations to recapitulate disease conditions or correct genetic defects, with resultant changes manifesting in altered insulin processing and cell stress responses. These genetically tailored models have shown promise in reversing diabetic phenotypes in preclinical transplantation studies, underscoring their therapeutic potential (maxwell2021applicationsofipscderived pages 4-5, oliveira2023type1diabetes pages 19-20).

Finally, high‑throughput screening‑compatible microplate assays have been integrated with these advanced systems to facilitate large‑scale drug testing. These assays employ sensitive detection methods to quantify insulin secretion dynamics and immune activation markers, allowing parallel evaluation of compound libraries under reproducible conditions. The integration of such assays with 3D culture platforms and microfluidic devices enhances the screening capacity by providing robust, quantitative readouts that correlate with in vivo beta‑cell function and immunogenicity (oliveira2023type1diabetes pages 10-12, rodriguezcomas2022isletonachipforthe pages 2-4).

Collectively, these sophisticated in vitro cell culture systems form a comprehensive and highly specific toolkit for Type 1 diabetes drug testing, enabling mechanistic insights and accelerating therapeutic development by closely mimicking human islet physiology and autoimmune interactions (thiab2025humaninducedpluripotentstem pages 26-27, oliveira2023type1diabetes pages 22-24).
